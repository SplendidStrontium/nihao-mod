## selectOrientations.py

corrected hardcoded directories on lines 15-16.

This script goes through the orientations that were previously generated in calcAxisRatios.py and decides which ten are most representative to move forward with. The ten orientations, as a restriction, is hardcoded on line 29. 

Note that it will select 10 orientations from whatever was generated by calcAxisRatios.py. Say, in this case, you only generated 11, as is the case with what I did. So selectOrientations makes 1.0 into 10 bins, and then picks whichever axisratio best matches the criteria of that bin. That means if you only generate, say, 11 axis ratios, you might have an uneven distribution, and your binning won't be great. So going forward, it may be advantageous to create a higher number of axis ratios, so that selectOrientations.py has a wider variety of numbers to select from, that is to say a likely more even distribution.

> python3 bin/selectOrientations.py --z=0 --galaxy=g15784

## bestParamsRT.py

RT = radiative transfer -> tracing photo packets as they move through the galaxy, simulating absorption and scattering to make synthetic observations. Notice that in line 36 it is loading particle data that was created at previous steps of the pipeline. It makes two runs of SKIRT, one with dust, and one without. 

There are lots of new parameters here, so let's review what needs to be set. Some were inputs to the makeParticles.py step, others are new.

--ageSmooth=False  # smooth stellar ages based on particle count?
--SF=False  # whether young stars (< 10 Myr) get special MAPPINGS-III SEDs to model star-forming regions
--tauClear=10  # the clearing timescale for those star-forming regions (only matters if SF is True)
--numPhotons=1e7  # how many photon packets SKIRT launches; more packets means less noise but longer runtime
--pixels=250  # img resolution
--dustFraction=0.3  # dust-to-metal ratio
--maxTemp=7e4  # maximum temperature at which dust can exist
--SSP=  # simple stellar population model & IMF; how does stellar mass become light output
--z=0  # redshift
--galaxy=g15784  # which galaxy we're working on

> python bin/bestParamsRT.py --ageSmooth=False --SF=False --tauClear=10 --numPhotons=1e7 --pixels=250 --dustFraction=0.3 --maxTemp=7e4 --SSP=Bpass_Chabrier --z=0 --galaxy=g15784
